Explain what a product backlog is and its purpose 

What is a Product Backlog? 

A Product Backlog is a prioritized list of all the work that needs to be done in an Agile project. 
It contains everything the product may require, including: 

Features 

Enhancements 

Bug fixes 

Technical tasks 

Research items 

The backlog is owned and managed by the Product Owner. 

Each item in the backlog is called a Product Backlog Item (PBI) or User Story. 

Purpose of the Product Backlog 

The Product Backlog serves several important purposes: 

1. Acts as the Single Source of Work 

It gathers all requirements in one place, ensuring the team knows exactly what needs to be built. 

 

2. Provides Prioritized Work 

Items are arranged by importance: 

High-value features appear at the top 

Less urgent items appear lower 

This helps the development team know what to focus on next. 

3. Supports Planning (Especially Sprint Planning) 

During Sprint Planning, the team selects the top backlog items to work on next. 
Without the backlog, planning would be impossible. 

4. Ensures Transparency 

All stakeholders (client, team, managers) can see: 

What is planned 

What is being worked on 

What remains 

This creates clarity and trust. 

 

5. Allows Continuous Adaptation 

The backlog is not fixed—it is updated regularly when: 

New requirements appear 

Priorities change 

Old items become irrelevant 

This supports Agile’s principle of embracing change. 

6. Helps the Team Deliver Maximum Value 

By focusing on the most important features first, the team ensures the product delivers value early and continuously.

#Explain backlog refinement and maintenance 

What is Backlog Refinement? 

Backlog Refinement (also called Backlog Grooming) is the ongoing process of reviewing, updating, and improving Product Backlog Items (PBIs) so that they are ready for future sprints. 

It is usually performed regularly, often once per sprint. 

What is Backlog Maintenance? 

Backlog Maintenance refers to the continuous effort of keeping the entire backlog healthy, updated, and aligned with product goals. 

If refinement is about improving items, maintenance is about keeping the whole backlog system under control.
